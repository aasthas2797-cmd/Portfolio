Climate Strategy & Risk

Knowing the risk is only half the answer.

What interests me is the harder question: what should a business actually do when every response involves a trade-off?

My Perspective

When I began studying sustainability, I was drawn to the frameworks and commitments. But the more I explored them, the more interested I became in the decisions that follow.

Consider a business exposed to flooding. Relocating may reduce physical risk but increase costs and disrupt suppliers. Staying may protect short-term operations while leaving the business vulnerable.

Neither answer is automatically right. That's what draws me to climate strategy: understanding the exposure, challenging the assumptions and finding a response the business can justify and deliver.

Circular Economy

Waste rarely begins where we notice it.

The question that has stayed with me is simple: why was that resource wasted in the first place?

My Perspective

Long before sustainability became a career interest, I was conscious of things that seemed unnecessarily wasted: electricity, water, plastic, or something thrown away when it could still be useful.

Working in operations gave that instinct a different perspective. By the time excess products become waste, the decisions responsible may have been made hours earlier through forecasting, purchasing or production planning.

That changed the question for me. Rather than only asking how waste can be managed, I became interested in how it could have been prevented, and whether doing so could make better business sense too.

Business Analysis & Operations

A good answer on paper can still fail in practice.

Working in operations changed how I judge a solution. Being analytically correct isn't quite enough.

My Perspective

I've seen how a perfectly reasonable plan can run into competing priorities, unexpected demand and limited resources. In those moments, the most useful person isn't necessarily the one with the most elaborate analysis. It's the one who can work out what matters, what can change and what needs to happen next.

That experience influences how I approach my independent projects too. I don't want to stop at identifying a problem. I want to understand its consequences, compare possible responses and test whether the recommendation still makes sense when the assumptions change.

My Story

I didn't always know I wanted to work in sustainability.

But I've always been conscious of the little things. Wasting electricity, using more water than necessary, throwing away something that could still be useful. And I'd often find myself encouraging people around me to think about these things too.

For a long time, I couldn't quite connect that interest to a career. Then came my master's.

Through the modules I studied, the projects I worked on and the ideas I presented, I began seeing a much bigger picture. What started as everyday awareness turned into questions I genuinely wanted answers to.

Why are things done this way? How do we change them? And what would it take to do things better?

The more I learned, the more curious I became. And I haven't stopped since.

Finding my way into this field hasn't been a straight road, but that hasn't made me want it any less.

I want to help shape business strategies, influence better environmental decisions and encourage people to be more conscious of their choices.

I didn't have it all figured out from the beginning. I still don't.

But I know this is a field I want to make my own.
